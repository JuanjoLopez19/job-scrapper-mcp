# Cover-letter writing guide

## Recommended shape

1. **Greeting** — name a verified recipient or use a neutral hiring-team greeting.
2. **Opening** — identify the exact role and give one specific reason for applying.
3. **Evidence** — connect one or two relevant achievements or experiences to the role's main needs.
4. **Contribution and motivation** — explain what the candidate can contribute and why the work is appealing, using only supported facts.
5. **Close** — thank the reader and invite a conversation without pressure.

## Evidence rules

- Candidate claims must come from the supplied CV, profile, or user statements.
- Employer claims must come from the retrieved offer or another clearly identified public source.
- A technology mentioned only in the offer is not a candidate skill.
- Do not manufacture metrics, enthusiasm, personal connections, culture fit, or years of experience.
- Prefer a modest concrete claim over an impressive vague one.

## Editing checklist

- Make the opening specific enough that it cannot be sent unchanged to another company.
- Give each paragraph one job.
- Prefer active verbs and plain language.
- Remove clichés such as "perfect fit", "dream company", and "I am writing to apply" when a more direct opening is possible.
- Vary sentence length and avoid starting every paragraph with "I".
- Keep visible keywords natural and readable; never insert hidden text or keyword lists.
- Preserve the user's voice when examples or prior writing are available.
